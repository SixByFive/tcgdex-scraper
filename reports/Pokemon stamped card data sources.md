# TCGdex plus TCGCSV and Cardmarket catalogue cover stamps

**No single source gives structured stamp names with Cardmarket, TCGplayer and CardTrader ids for 2020-2026 Pokémon sets, but TCGdex comes closest, and two free feeds fill most of its gaps.** TCGdex is the only free, unauthenticated source that models stamps as a first-class field (`variants_detailed[].stamp[]`, about 130 enumerated values). It attaches a per-variant Cardmarket id to most stamped variants and a TCGplayer id to few. Its coverage is hand-curated and uneven: it reports zero stamped variants for sv01, swsh1, swsh12.5, ME05 and (a week after release) ME06, although stamped printings very likely exist for several of these ([TCGdex API](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/en/cards/sv08.5-082)). TCGCSV, the free daily mirror of TCGplayer, supplies TCGplayer product ids with the stamp written in the product name. It covers promo-numbered printings (Prerelease, Staff, Pokémon Center Exclusive, "Pitch Black Stamped", Prize Pack) but not set-logo stamped copies of main-set cards ([TCGCSV](https://tcgcsv.com/tcgplayer/3/groups)). Cardmarket's open catalogue download carries every stamped product id but no stamp text, so it can only flag candidates. CardTrader has no usable public stamp field. pokemontcg.io has no stamp or variant data at all, and Scrydex, PokeData, JustTCG and PriceCharting are paid or restricted with no confirmed stamp field. The recommendation is to keep TCGdex as the stamp-name and Cardmarket-id backbone. Add a TCGCSV name parser for TCGplayer ids and promo stamps, use the Cardmarket catalogue as a gap detector, and use Bulbapedia's prerelease table as a validation list. CardTrader should wait for a real API token.

Most evidence below was fetched live on 2026-11-14 by the note authors. The notes explicitly mark what was only seen in search snippets or inferred, and this report preserves that distinction. Several sites (Bulbapedia in one note, TCGplayer, CardTrader, tcgcollector, Cardmarket's website) blocked automated fetching, so the claims about them are thinner.

## TCGdex is the only stamp-native source, and it is sparse where reality is dense

TCGdex's card endpoint returns `variants_detailed[]` with `type`, `size`, `stamp[]`, `foil`, `variantId`, per-variant `thirdParty{cardmarket,tcgplayer}` and pricing. For Lugia ex in Prismatic Evolutions (sv08.5-082), the plain holo carries Cardmarket 805474 and TCGplayer 610437. Four set-logo variants (normal, normal jumbo, holo, holo jumbo) carry Cardmarket ids 841280, 841281, 868293 and 868296, with TCGplayer null ([TCGdex API](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/en/cards/sv08.5-082)). The data is MIT-licensed, lives as hand-written TypeScript files in a GitHub repo, and can be built offline ([cards-database](https://github.com/tcgdex/cards-database)). Maintenance is current: commits on 2026-11-13 added a "Game On" stamp and fixed Delta Reign Cardmarket ids ([commits](https://api.github.com/repos/tcgdex/cards-database/commits?path=data&per_page=8)). New sets appear about a day after release (Delta Reign on 2026-11-07), but stamps and ids arrive later. Ascended Heroes got its extra ME variant types about 2.5 months after release, and Phantasmal Flames got TCGplayer ids about 3.5 months after.

A full-catalogue scan on 2026-11-14 shows how real the stamp data is where it exists. Prismatic Evolutions has 16 set-logo cards, one snowflake and one 30th-pokeday. The promo sets are dense: SVP has 75 set-logo, 39 staff and 19 pokemon-center cards, and MEP has 36 set-logo, 21 staff and 8 pokemon-center. Roughly half of all promo-set entries are stamped. For main sets the figure is about 5-10% of cards (this proportion is the note author's inference from the scan). The stamp vocabulary covers every category the task lists, including set-logo, pre-release, pokemon-center, staff, the four league-ball tiers, player-rewards-program, professor-program, snowflake, worlds-20xx, gamestop, eb-games, trick-or-trade and 30th-pokeday ([interfaces.d.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tcgdex/cards-database/master/interfaces.d.ts)).

Four weaknesses matter for the pipeline.

1. **Zero-stamp sets.** The ground-truth scan finds no stamped variants for swsh1, swsh3.5, swsh4.5, swsh12.5, sv01, me05 and me06. Independent evidence says stamped printings exist at least for Pitch Black, because TCGplayer lists "Slowbro - 083 (Pitch Black Stamped)" in the MEP group ([TCGCSV MEP group](https://tcgcsv.com/tcgplayer/24451/products)). A zero should therefore be read as "unknown", not "none exist". The cards-database notes make the same inference.
2. **Missing marketplace ids.** TCGplayer ids on stamped variants are absent in every Scarlet & Violet set measured through Destined Rivals and only appear from White Flare onward (White Flare 3 of 3, Black Bolt 19 of 20, ME02 4 of 4, ME02.5 9 of 9, ME03 11 of 24, ME04 2 of 4). Cardmarket ids are missing on 33 of 46 stamped variants in sv09, 34 of 41 in me01 and 13 of 24 in me03, mostly the Play! Pokémon prize-pack entries. Whether Cardmarket simply does not list them separately is the note author's inference.
3. **Bulk access.** The REST set endpoint lists cards without variants, so stamps need one request per card. GraphQL accepted the `variants_detailed` selection but returned null for every sv08.5 card. The docs state no rate limit, so the notes' scans ran at about 8 requests per second without trouble. The alternative is building the JSON locally from the repo. The `variantId` is an opaque hash whose stability was not tested, so join on set, local number, type, size and stamp array instead.
4. **Naming quirks.** `set-logo` is a catch-all. HGSS-onward prerelease stamps carry the expansion logo, and `pre-release` is used only for 3 sv03 cards, so a marketplace "(Prerelease)" label will not match by name alone. The label `30th-pokeday` also sits on a 2025 Prismatic Evolutions Sylveon that other sources call a Pokémon Day 2025 card ([Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/all-pokemon-day-2025-promo-cards-and-how-to-get-them)). The ground-truth note treats that as a likely TCGdex naming quirk, which is an inference.

## TCGCSV gives TCGplayer ids and promo stamps, but not set-logo copies

TCGCSV is a free, unauthenticated JSON mirror of TCGplayer's catalogue, refreshed daily around 20:00 UTC. It exposes 221 Pokémon groups with product ids, names, `extendedData` (Number, Rarity) and prices. A full sync is about 222 requests, and the author fetched all 221 product lists with 8 parallel workers without trouble ([TCGCSV docs](https://tcgcsv.com/docs)). The usage rules are polite ones: sync once per 24 hours, check `last-updated.txt` first, send a custom User-Agent, sleep about 100 ms between calls, and avoid user-facing calls. There is no formal terms of service.

TCGplayer does list stamps as separate products, but only for promo-numbered printings. In the SV promo group 22872 the product names carry bracket tags: Prerelease 57, Staff 39, Pokemon Center Exclusive 16, plus World Championships 2023/24/25 and Illustration Contest 2024 ([SV promo group](https://tcgcsv.com/tcgplayer/3/22872/products)). Examples are "Quaquaval - 005 (Prerelease)" (487751) and "Tinkaton - 020 (Prerelease) [Staff]" (512506). The SWSH promo group has "Dragonite (Silver Tempest Stamped)" (547707) and "Professor Burnet - SWSH167 (25th Anniversary Stamp)". The MEP group 24451 has "Alakazam - 009 (Pokemon Center Exclusive)" (656261) and "Dhelmise - 084 (Prerelease)" (706137). The Prize Pack group 22880 holds main-set cards with series marks such as "(Prize Pack Series 6)" and "(Left Stamp)". The name grammar is `{Name} - {number} ({program}) [{staff-or-placement}]`, with spelling drift ("Cosmo" and "Cosmos" Holo, "(Staff)", "[Staff]", "[STAFF]", mojibake in "Pokémon").

The important negative result is that **no 2020+ main-set group contains a set-logo stamped duplicate**. Prismatic Evolutions group 23821 (398 products) has only sealed Pokémon Center, Dollar General and Sam's Club products plus "(Poke Ball Pattern)" and "(Master Ball Pattern)" cards, which are foil patterns, not stamps, and a single Lugia ex. This is the note author's scan of product names, not a read of TCGplayer's SKU "printing" data, which TCGCSV omits. So the four stamped Lugia ids in TCGdex have no TCGplayer counterpart to find. The official TCGplayer API is not an alternative, since TCGplayer says it is "no longer granting new API access" (reported from search summaries, with the closure date disputed) ([TCGplayer docs](https://docs.tcgplayer.com/docs/getting-started)).

## Cardmarket's catalogue finds every stamped product but cannot name the stamp

Cardmarket's open S3 downloads (`products_singles_6.json`, 75,695 products on 2026-11-12, and `price_guide_6.json`) returned 200 to plain requests ([catalogue](https://downloads.s3.cardmarket.com/productCatalog/productList/products_singles_6.json)). Product fields are only idProduct, name, idCategory, idExpansion, idMetacard and dateAdded. There is no stamp or version field, and the 326 names that matched stamp-like words were all attack names. A stamped printing is a separate idProduct with an identical name. Lugia ex has eight such products, four of them in "Prismatic Evolutions: Additionals" (expansion 6009).

The catalogue still helps in three ways. Cardmarket creates "<Set>: Additionals" expansions (6009 PRE, 6290 Mega Evolution, 6300 Phantasmal Flames, 6140 Destined Rivals, 6455 Ascended Heroes) that hold stamped duplicates and reverse-foil patterns. TCGdex's hand-kept `cm_expansions.ts` maps these ids to names, and this is the only expansion-name source found ([cm_expansions.ts](https://github.com/tcgdex/cards-database/blob/master/scripts/utils-data/cm_expansions.ts)). Stamped products sit in at least four kinds of expansion: the main set, Additionals, the Black Star promo sets and dedicated sets such as Prize Pack Series and Trick or Trade. So idExpansion alone does not identify a stamp. On the Lugia test, grouping by idMetacard and name, taking the main-set product as base, and clustering extras by dateAdded and idProduct adjacency reproduced TCGdex's four set-logo ids exactly. That test covered one card, and the author calls the output "candidates for manual review". Additionals also hold Poké Ball and Master Ball reverse foils, which are not stamps.

Access and licence are the weak point. The official Cardmarket API is closed to new applications, the credentials may not be shared with third-party apps ([Cardmarket help](https://help.cardmarket.com/en/cardmarket-api)), and the website returned 403 to automated fetches. No licence statement exists for the catalogue download, though TCGdex's public scripts already use it, which is precedent but not permission.

CardTrader deserves a caveat. Its Pokémon catalogue splits Prismatic Evolutions Poké Ball and Master Ball patterns into separate expansions, and the user's earlier TCGTracking finding was a single blueprint (316695) for PRE Lugia ex. Stamped printings are therefore probably filed either in the promo expansions or in separate blueprints named with a stamp suffix. That is an inference. No Pokémon blueprint JSON with a stamp field was found, and field-level details, rate limits and API terms could not be read.

## Where the notes disagree, and how to resolve it

First, the notes conflict over Bulbapedia access. The ground-truth note reports 403s and Cloudflare challenges on all Bulbapedia variants including the MediaWiki API, and used only snippets. The CardTrader and fan-database note reports that `api.php` with `action=parse&prop=wikitext` worked with a plain User-Agent and read the Prerelease cards page in full. The second result is more specific and is the better evidence, but a fresh test with the pipeline's own User-Agent should decide it.

Second, the notes differ on which TCGdex sets have stamps. The ground-truth scan lists stamps for sv02 through sv06 (for example sv03 with pre-release 3, set-logo 3, player-rewards-program 10 and 5 trick-or-trade, and sv05 with 24 player-rewards-program and international-championship staff and champion variants). The card-API note says sv02-sv06 were "still scanning" and generalises that set-logo stamps are under-represented for sv01-sv06. It also says `pre-release` and Pokémon Center stamps appear in no measured main set, which holds only for the sets it measured and is contradicted by sv03. The ground-truth scan is the more complete one and should be preferred. Both agree that sv01, swsh1 and swsh12.5 have zero.

Third, they disagree on Prismatic Evolutions counts: a retailer lists a "complete set of 9" PRE stamped promos ([Solesavy](https://store.solesavy.com/products/2025-pokemon-pre-en-prismatic-evolutions-stamped-promo-cards-complete-set-of-9-ungraded-card)), whereas TCGdex has 16 set-logo cards. The likely explanation is that TCGdex includes Surprise Box, Premium and Special Collection stamps beyond the 9, but no source resolves it.

Fourth, the sv10 Destined Rivals GameStop gap is unresolved. TCGdex has a GameStop stamp in neighbouring sets but none in sv10, and the note author could not tell whether this is a data gap or the end of GameStop's US program. The Bulbapedia prerelease table does agree with TCGdex on the Destined Rivals stamped set cards (034, 049, 087, 096).

Fifth, the TCGplayer and Cardmarket notes agree in direction but describe different universes. TCGCSV shows promo-numbered stamped cards (SVP "(Prerelease)" and MEP "Pitch Black Stamped") with real TCGplayer ids, while TCGdex's Cardmarket ids for set-logo stamps live in "Additionals" expansions that TCGplayer never mirrors. The two sources are complementary, not redundant. One more mismatch: TCGplayer's SVP group has 57 Prerelease-tagged products, whereas TCGdex labels most prerelease cards `set-logo` and only 3 `pre-release`, so the two vocabularies need an explicit mapping table.

## Recommended stack and integration plan

Keep the existing pokemon.com scrape and TCGTracking as the base card and id spine. Treat TCGdex `variants_detailed` as the primary stamp source. The recommended additions, in priority order, are the following.

1. **Parse TCGCSV promo groups.** Pull groups for the SWSH, SV and ME promo sets and the Prize Pack, Battle Academy and League/Championship groups. Match on group, normalised `extendedData.Number` and the name before " - ". Map "(Prerelease)" to prerelease, "[Staff]" to staff, "(Pokemon Center Exclusive)" to pokemon-center, "(X Stamped)" to a set-logo stamp for the named set, and "(Prize Pack Series N)" to player-rewards-program. Normalise the spelling variants listed above. Compare TCGTracking's own coverage against these groups first, since TCGTracking's group count (221) matches TCGCSV's and the notes did not check whether it keeps the promo groups.
2. **Merge by (setId, localId, type, size, stamp set).** When TCGdex and TCGCSV agree on a promo variant, attach the TCGplayer id to the TCGdex variant. Where TCGCSV has a stamped product that TCGdex lacks, create a candidate variant flagged `source=tcgcsv`. Never overwrite a TCGdex stamp with a TCGCSV tag without a mapping-table hit.
3. **Run a Cardmarket gap detector.** Download the catalogue and expansion map, group by idMetacard and name, and list products that no TCGdex variant references. These are candidates for missing stamped variants (including the zero-stamp sets), and a human or the Bulbapedia list names them. Treat the output as unconfirmed, because Additionals also hold foil-pattern duplicates.
4. **Cross-check with Bulbapedia.** Parse the prerelease table (MEP 001-004 Mega Evolution, 014-017 Phantasmal Flames, 064-067 Perfect Order, 074-077 Chaos Rising, 082-085 Pitch Black, 111-114 Delta Reign, plus the SVP ranges) to confirm which events have stamped prerelease printings. This will flag ME05 and ME06 as expected but missing in TCGdex. Cite and keep the CC BY-NC-SA terms in mind before redistributing derived text.
5. **Add CardTrader only with a token.** Fetch `/expansions` and `/blueprints/export` for the promo expansions and the Pokémon pattern expansions, filter names for "Stamp", "Pre-Release" and "Staff", and read `card_market_ids` and `tcg_player_id` for cross-validation. Verify the token process and terms first, because the docs were unreadable.
6. **Skip** pokemontcg.io (deprecated, no stamps), PokeData (redistribution banned), Limitless and pkmncards (no stamp data). Evaluate Scrydex or JustTCG only if a paid or free key can first confirm stamped variants for a known card such as sv08.5-082.

Operationally, cache TCGdex per-card responses and diff them daily, since stamps and ids arrive in follow-up pull requests. Contribute missing variants back upstream where possible, because that is where TCGdex's coverage is actually produced. Keep foil patterns (Poké Ball, Master Ball, peelable-ditto) out of stamp counts. Flag `toys-r-us` and `build-a-bear` in the schema as likely legacy placeholders, since TCGdex has zero occurrences and the notes found no evidence for either in 2020-2026 (absence of evidence only, not confirmed). The stamp list also needs `pokemon-day` and the international-championship and named-player stamps that TCGdex carries.

## Conclusion

The practical finding is that "stamped" splits into two data problems that need different sources. Promo-numbered stamped cards (prerelease, staff, Pokémon Center, worlds, prize pack) are well represented as TCGplayer products with the stamp in the name, so TCGCSV can supply those ids cheaply. Set-logo stamped copies of main-set cards exist only as Cardmarket ids and in TCGdex's hand-curated stamp field, so no automated source can name them. A Cardmarket-based gap detector, checked against Bulbapedia, is the only scalable way to find what TCGdex is missing.

What remains unverified limits how far to trust the plan. TCGplayer's SKU-level printing data, Scrydex and JustTCG schemas, CardTrader's blueprint stamp handling, tcgcollector's data access and the Cardmarket website's version labels were all out of reach. Before committing engineering time, run three short tests: a keyed Scrydex or JustTCG lookup of sv08.5-082, a CardTrader token pull of the promo expansions, and a Bulbapedia API call from the pipeline's own network path.
