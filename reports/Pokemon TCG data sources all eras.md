# Stack TCGdex, Bulbapedia and official Asian databases

For sets missing from pokemon.com, the best-supported plan is a layered stack. Use **TCGdex** (MIT, 17 language endpoints, already carrying Wizards sets, POP, McDonald's, trainer kits, Jumbo, Sample, Best of Game, Rumble and Poké Card Creator Pack in English) as the id and name backbone. Add **PokemonTCG/pokemon-tcg-data** for English stats (HP, retreat, artist, rarity, attacks), and use **Bulbapedia via the MediaWiki API** for promo distribution and sealed-product contents. For Asian languages, go to the official databases (**pokemon-card.com**, **asia.pokemon-card.com**, **pokemoncard.co.kr**), which cover the modern era without a bot wall in light probing. Each layer has a gap. TCGdex's vintage non-English data and its Asian sets are thin or have suspect ids. pokemon-tcg-data is frozen because the live API shuts down on 1 March 2027. Wizards-era sealed contents and Japanese sealed contents have **no verified structured source**. Most findings below come from light probing through summarising fetch tools, so treat coverage claims as leads to confirm before you commit engineering time. The final section separates what was checked from what was not.

## What to do for sets not on pokemon.com

The French pokemon.com card encyclopedia lists series only from EX onward (Méga-Évolution back to EX, with no Neo, Base or e-Card) ([pokemon.com/fr](https://www.pokemon.com/fr/jcc-pokemon/cartes-pokemon/)). That matches the user's premise, but the researcher only confirmed it for French. DE, IT, ES and PT pages were not fetched, and the guessed German URL returned 404. The pokemon.com site is therefore the wrong primary source for older sets, and promo lines, McDonald's, trainer kits and similar programs need other sources.

The most useful finding is that **TCGdex already holds an entry for nearly every set in question**. Its English set list includes base1-5, gym1-2, neo1-4, si1, Legendary Collection (`lc`), ecard1-3, `basep`, `wp`, `np`, `dpp`, `hgssp`, `bwp`, `xyp`, `smp`, `swshp`, `svp`, `mep`, `jumbo` (160 cards), `sp` (Sample), `bog` (Best of Game), `miscp`, `ex5.5` (Poké Card Creator Pack), `exu` (Unown Collection), pop1-9, `ru1` (Rumble), Radiant Collection, McDonald's yearly sets, Futsal and 20 trainer kits ([TCGdex EN sets](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/en/sets), [TCGdex trainer kits](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/en/series/tk)). The practical question is therefore not where to find these sets but how complete each entry is. Only base1-4 and `basep` were inspected at card level. The researchers did not audit stats for Jumbo, Sample, `miscp`, the HS/BW/XY/SM trainer kits or McDonald's 2017-2024.

For enrichment, `pokemon-tcg-data` carries full per-card fields for base1-5, gym, neo, e-Card, `si1`, `basep`, `bp`, `np`, `dpp`, `hsp`, the EX trainer kits, POP, Rumble, McDonald's 2011-2016/2021-22, Futsal and Celebrations ([sets/en.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PokemonTCG/pokemon-tcg-data/master/sets/en.json), [base1.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PokemonTCG/pokemon-tcg-data/master/cards/en/base1.json)). Its set list reportedly omits `wp`, Jumbo, Sample, `miscp`, Poké Card Creator, Unown Collection and Radiant Collection, but that absence came from a summary that filtered by id prefix, so check it directly. For sets neither source covers in depth, PkmnCards (sortable by HP, rarity, retreat and illustrator, robots.txt blocks only `?s=` searches) and Pokellector (no robots.txt) are scrapable but have no export and unread terms ([PkmnCards](https://pkmncards.com/set/base-set/), [robots.txt](https://pkmncards.com/robots.txt), [Pokellector](https://www.pokellector.com/Base-Set-Expansion/)).

Joining sources is where the real work lies. Ids differ: TCGdex `lc` is `base6` in pokemon-tcg-data, `bog` is `bp`, `hgssp` is `hsp`, `2011bw` is `mcd11`, `tk-ex-latia` is `tk1a`, and `fut2020` is `fut20`. Totals differ for legitimate reasons (secret rares past the printed number: Aquapolis 147/182, Skyridge 144/182). TCGdex splits Unown Collection from Unseen Forces while pokemon-tcg-data appears to fold it in (145 total versus TCGdex's 117 plus a separate 28), and TCGdex's ecard2 count (186) is four above pokemon-tcg-data's 182 and needs investigation ([sets/en.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PokemonTCG/pokemon-tcg-data/master/sets/en.json), [TCGdex EN sets](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/en/sets)). A per-set alias table is unavoidable.

## Source matrix by era and language

The table summarises what the notes support. "TCGdex sets" are live counts from 2026-11-20 ([TCGdex live API](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/en/sets), per [apis_and_aggregators note](https://tcgdex.dev/)). The "Status" column says how well it is verified.

| Era and language | Best sources | Status |
|---|---|---|
| EN Wizards (Base to e-Card, `si1`, `lc`) | TCGdex (variants such as 1st edition and shadowless) plus pokemon-tcg-data (stats) | Verified at card level for base1-4 only |
| EN EX to modern, promos, McDonald's, kits | TCGdex (221 sets), pokemon-tcg-data, TCGCSV groups for TCGplayer ids | Set lists verified; card fields mostly not |
| JA from 1996 (PMCG1) | TCGdex ja (204 sets, name-only for vintage), pokemon-card.com JSON (23,640 cards, apparently from about the PCG/DP era onward), Bulbapedia era lists | TCGdex and JSON endpoint verified; official DB's earliest era unconfirmed |
| JA vintage images and rarity | None found | Open gap |
| KO | pokemoncard.co.kr (robots allows all), TCGdex ko (95 sets, listing ended at SV6), type-null/PTCG-database | Partly verified |
| zh-TW, zh-HK, TH, ID | asia.pokemon-card.com (one platform, region segment in URL), TCGdex (98, none, 72, 70) | Site pages read; no JSON endpoint checked; HK not probed on TCGdex |
| zh-CN (mainland) | TCGdex (57 sets, duplicate and mixed ids), Bulbapedia "(ATCG)" set pages, CardTrader, fan guide; pokemon.cn has no card database | Weak; ids need validating |
| DE, FR | TCGdex (DE 157, FR 203 sets), PokéWiki.de (CC BY-SA), Poképédia, Pokécardex, PkmCards.fr | Lists read; MediaWiki API not tested |
| IT, ES, PT | TCGdex (IT 194, ES 160, PT 126; PT vintage starts at base1-3 then thins) | Thin and unevenly verified |
| NL, PL, RU | TCGdex has 3, 2 and 9 sets; forum claims only | Very weak |

Several non-obvious points follow from this table. In Asia, TW, HK, TH, ID and KO reuse Japanese set codes (SV2a is the 151 set in all five), so a shared code join works there ([TCGdex TH](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/th/sets), [TCGdex ID](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/id/sets), [TCGdex ZH-TW](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/zh-tw/sets)). Mainland China uses its own CS, CSM and CSV codes with a trailing C and needs a hand-built mapping ([fan guide](https://sites.google.com/view/chinsespokemonsets)). TCGdex data quality in Asia is a real risk. The note flags CSV1C appearing twice, Taiwan-style ids on the zh-cn list, and a Traditional Chinese name ("三連音爆", Triple Beat) repeated across several CS ids with 101 cards each ([TCGdex zh-cn](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/zh-cn/sets), [TCGdex zh-tw](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/zh-tw/sets)). The Thai and Indonesian lists show the same repeated-name pattern. Validate those against the official Asia portal before trusting them.

For Japan, the official JSON endpoint `resultAPI.php` lists cards with stable numeric ids and image paths, but returns only id, thumbnail and name. Number, rarity (an icon only) and set code require a detail-page fetch per card ([resultAPI](https://www.pokemon-card.com/card-search/resultAPI.php?keyword=&regulation_sidebar_form=all&page=1), [detail page](https://www.pokemon-card.com/card-search/details.php/card/50856/regu/all)). The `type-null/PTCG-database` project (MIT, 172,361 cards, 11 languages, runs through September 2026) already combines the official JP, KO and Asia sites with TCGdex and other sources, and it throttles to about one request per second with backoff on 403 and 429. It is a ready reference implementation and possibly a data source ([type-null/PTCG-database](https://github.com/type-null/PTCG-database)). It says its "Simplified Chinese" text is really Taiwan's text under Japanese codes.

For European languages, set names and dates lag English and some sets never shipped in German (Base Set 2, Gym Heroes, Gym Challenge, Legendary Collection) ([PokéWiki Erweiterungen](https://pokewiki.de/Erweiterungen)). Localized numbering and rarity must be read from the localized set page rather than copied from English. Edition markers for localized vintage sets were not found on the PokéWiki or Poképédia pages checked.

## Promo and sealed-product sources

**Promos.** Bulbapedia is the only source found that gives per-card distribution (product, event and date), organised per promo set and per language, in parseable wikitext (`{{Setlist/entry|…}}`) ([Bulbapedia hub via API](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/w/api.php?action=parse&page=Promotional_cards_(TCG)&prop=wikitext&format=json)). Its hub covers Wizards, Nintendo, DP, HGSS, BW, XY, SM, SWSH and SVP Black Star lines, McDonald's 2011-2024, Jumbo, Prerelease, Futsal and POP. It also has Japanese promo series pages (PCG-P through M-P) and Traditional Chinese, Simplified Chinese, Korean, Thai and Indonesian pages. The ~57 KB "Unnumbered Promotional cards" page is the only machine-readable source found for tournament-placement and unnumbered promos ([Unnumbered page](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Unnumbered_Promotional_cards_(TCG))). Counts line up where checked: SVP 226 and SWSH 307 match TCGdex. For Japanese promos TCGdex is sparse: only `SV-P` (288 versus Bulbapedia's 304), `M-P`, `SV2P`, `S10P` and `SMP2` exist, and `SM-P`, `S-P`, `XY-P`, `BW-P`, `L-P`, `PLAY` and `DP-P` returned 404 ([TCGdex ja sets](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/ja/sets), [TCGdex ja SV-P](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/ja/sets/SV-P)). TCGCSV helps with the many non-numbered programs. Its Pokémon category has groups for League & Championship, Jumbo (347 products), Deck Exclusives, Prize Pack, Professor Program, Burger King, Blister Exclusives, Southeast Asia Exclusives, Trick or Trade and Battle Academy ([TCGCSV groups](https://tcgcsv.com/tcgplayer/3/groups)). Jumbo and Staff versions are folded into one row in Bulbapedia but split into separate sets in TCGdex and TCGCSV, so counts will not match. TCGdex lacks a 2013 McDonald's set and a 2026 one, while Bulbapedia lists both and TCGCSV has 2026 ([TCGdex mc series](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/en/series/mc)).

**Sealed products.** Contents data is the weakest part of the research. The best verified contents source is again Bulbapedia, which has one merchandise page per series and language (Original through Mega Evolution, with FR, DE, IT, PT and zh-TW variants). Its Scarlet & Violet page records pack counts and sets, promo card ids with finish and jumbo flags, code cards and accessories ([Bulbapedia SV merchandise via API](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/w/api.php?action=parse&page=Scarlet_%26_Violet_TCG_Series_merchandise&prop=wikitext&format=json)). It is wikitext with free-text pack descriptions that need normalising. A forum user called the EX page the best sealed database they know of, while another said it is "vastly lacking in promotional sealed product" ([Elite Fourum](https://www.elitefourum.com/t/sealed-product-database/23572)). Wizards-era pages looked thin from search snippets and were not read directly. Marketplace sources give product lists, ids and prices but little structure. TCGCSV product records have free-text `CardText` that describes contents (an ETB lists 8 packs, 65 sleeves and so on) and often a UPC, as seen on the Silver Tempest group ([TCGCSV group 3170](https://tcgcsv.com/tcgplayer/3/3170/products)). TCGTracking adds `/sealed` and `/skus` endpoints with language values and Cardmarket and CardTrader ids ([TCGTracking](https://openapi.tcgtracking.com/)). Cardmarket publishes a downloadable product catalogue including non-singles ([Cardmarket news](https://news.cardmarket.com/en/FoW/adding-non-singles-and-accessories-to-the-price-guide)). Pokémon's POK-prefixed SKUs have no public database ([Elite Fourum](https://www.elitefourum.com/t/pokemon-productcode-database/28089)). For TCGplayer, TCGCSV Pokémon Japan (category 85, 463 groups) covers Japanese products, but nothing about its sealed contents was sampled. Official product pages (pokemon.com's gallery shows only image and name; pokemon-card.com's `/products/` shows name, thumbnail and description) carry no verified contents ([pokemon.com gallery](https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-tcg/product-gallery), [pokemon-card.com](https://www.pokemon-card.com/products/)). TCGdex has no sealed data. The Asia portal lists decks and special sets as products with codes, but no contents were found in any source for KO, zh, TH or ID. For a data model, the one open precedent is MTGJSON's MIT-licensed `mtg-sealed-content`, whose `pack`, `deck`, `sealed`, `variable`, `card` and `other` content types map well to Pokémon ([mtg-sealed-content](https://github.com/mtgjson/mtg-sealed-content)). No Pokémon equivalent was found.

## Recommendations, access rules and licences

Build in this order. First, vendor `pokemon-tcg-data` now and mirror TCGdex's `data` and `data-asia` folders (MIT, self-hostable). The pokemontcg.io API is deprecated, shuts down on 1 March 2027, and returned HTTP 500 and 502 during testing ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PokemonTCG/pokemon-tcg-data/master/README.md)). Second, write the alias and crosswalk table (TCGdex set id to pokemon-tcg-data id to TCGplayer groupId to Bulbapedia page title). TCGdex card variants already carry Cardmarket and TCGplayer product ids at card level, and a set-level crosswalk can be derived by aggregating them, though nobody ran that ([TCGdex swsh3-136](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/en/cards/swsh3-136)). Third, parse Bulbapedia promo and merchandise wikitext through the API, with a script User-Agent and cached, low-volume calls. HTML pages and WebFetch returned 403, but about 25 API calls were not blocked, and robots.txt allows `/wiki/` ([robots.txt](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/robots.txt)). Fourth, extend the existing scraper's regional logic to the Asia portal (one scraper, region parameter) and `pokemoncard.co.kr`, using type-null's pacing as a template. Fifth, prefer TCGdex's Japanese names for vintage JA sets and accept name-only data until an image and rarity source is found.

On licences, only TCGdex is clearly open (MIT), and PokéWiki is CC BY-SA. Bulbapedia's CC BY-NC-SA licence was not re-verified, so use it as a lookup and validation layer with attribution rather than copying wholesale. PriceCharting forbids redistribution. TCGCSV and TCGTracking state no terms and derive from TCGplayer and CardTrader data, so treat prices and product text from them as non-redistributable until checked. Pokedata's API terms prohibit redistribution and competing use ([Pokedata terms](https://www.pokedata.io/api-terms)). Scrydex starts at $29 per month and is the named successor to pokemontcg.io, but its coverage and redistribution terms were not read ([Scrydex pricing](https://scrydex.com/pricing)). Image and card-text rights belong to the IP owners regardless of the data licence, and that was not researched for TCGdex assets.

## What was verified and what remains open

**Verified directly** (live probe or page read on 2026-11-20): TCGdex set lists and counts for en, fr, de, it, pt, ja, ko, zh-tw, zh-cn, th and id; TCGdex card records for base1-4, swsh3-136 and ja SV-P-001; the `resultAPI.php` JSON structure at pokemon-card.com; Korean robots.txt; the pokemon-tcg-data set list and sample card; TCGCSV group lists and the Silver Tempest product records; the Bulbapedia API for promo, hub and Scarlet & Violet merchandise pages. **Verified only through summarising fetch tools or search snippets** (so field lists and counts are approximate): the Asia portal pages, the French pokemon.com page, PokéWiki, Poképédia, Pokécardex, PkmnCards, Pokellector, Serebii link lists, pokumon.com and most pricing-API docs. **Not verified or blocked**: Cardmarket (403), Bulbapedia HTML (403), Bulbapedia language and other-region expansion lists, Wizards, Neo, e-Card and Japanese merchandise pages, and all terms-of-service text except a few robots.txt files.

The key open gaps, in order of impact, are these. (1) **Sealed contents** have no source for Wizards-era products (the Original-series Bulbapedia page was only seen in snippets, and the research found the `{{TCGMerch}}` links but not their targets), Japanese products (High Class, Special Box, starter decks, Pokémon Center exclusives), or any Asian-language market. (2) **Vintage Japanese images and rarity** have no source, and TCGdex PMCG1 has name-only cards with variant counts that do not reconcile. (3) **TCGdex card-level quality** outside base1-4 and the modern JA set is unsampled, and its Asian id and name problems are only flagged. (4) **European localized vintage** coverage in IT, ES and PT is thin, and DE, IT, ES and PT pokemon.com pages, URL patterns and bot behaviour were not tested live. (5) **Official-site details** are unknown: pokemon-card.com's earliest era and promo list, the Asia and Korean sites' JSON endpoints, rate limits and terms, and the Indonesian site. (6) **Regional promos** (Korea, China, Taiwan, Thailand, Indonesia, Europe), event and magazine promos, and 2010-2018 English tin contents were not compared card by card. (7) **Licences and rate limits** for most sources are unknown. Not researched at all: Serebii per-card distribution text, Limitless's per-language set pages, Scrydex coverage, and Asian marketplace sources (Snkrdunk, Cardrush, Danawa, Shopee and others).

## Conclusion

The research reframes the user's question. The sets missing from pokemon.com are mostly not missing from open data. TCGdex already lists them, and the work is quality auditing and id reconciliation rather than discovery. The genuinely unsolved problems are narrower than the brief implied: sealed contents for pre-EX English products and for all Japanese and other Asian products, vintage Japanese images and rarity, and Italian, Spanish and Portuguese vintage data.

Two near-term decisions follow. Snapshot pokemon-tcg-data before the 1 March 2027 shutdown, and sample 5 to 10 cards from each non-pokemon.com TCGdex set (Jumbo, Sample, trainer kits, McDonald's 2017-2024, Japanese promos) to learn which sets need a second source, before building scrapers for all of them. A separate report on stamped-card data sources (`H:\tcgdex-scraper\reports\Pokemon stamped card data sources.md`) covers stamp mechanics and was not repeated here.
